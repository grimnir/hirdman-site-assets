Where one leaked login leads

Follow one breached login through the rounds our analysts run.

1

A breach lists one corporate login. The password was changed and the ticket closed. We take the identifiers out of the record: login, mailbox name, password pattern, unique domain.

2

Round one: the same mailbox name turns up on a personal address and at a previous employer.

3

Round two: a username that mutates by the same rule, and a shop registered with the work email and the same password.

4

Round three: the shop's password opens the corporate portal, and an admin login without email appears. Reverse checks are built to find this path.

5

Every link gets a fix and a priority: unique passwords, passkeys and a second factor on the portal, the admin account closed. Read the full explanation.

From a recent engagement

Case: live keys in a public file

During attack-surface reconnaissance we found a UI development tool on two of the client's subdomains. Its version compiled the environment file into the public bundle: tokens for repositories, cloud roles, databases and storage.

The domain had zero leaked emails or passwords; attack-surface reconnaissance is what found the exposure. CVSS rates the vulnerability only Medium (5.3), yet the file held live keys. One token confirmed repository access. We notified the client at once, and rotation of every secret started right after.

Questions about the free check

What do you need from us to start?

A company domain and a contact. We work only with data that has already leaked: it circulates on the internet and counts as compromised, and anyone set on attacking you can take the same steps. We keep the data exactly as it leaked, use it only to assess your risk and never sell it. What we add is speed and scale: we process terabytes of data and link the records, and the links show habits, human weaknesses, password reuse and patterns. We assess risk on that basis.

We test passwords for validity and for 2FA bypass only after a contract is signed, or under a signed proof of concept if you ask for one.

How long does it take?

An attacker's initial reconnaissance takes about 30 minutes on average, and our first picture comes as fast. Within half an hour of tracing the links we can see how well the company is protected: whether it has security at all, how strong it is, what its digital hygiene is like and whether security awareness is in place. We call it the eagle-eye view.

For a proof of concept, the surface report takes 1 to 2 days. The full work depends on the plan and the task: leak assessment, insider search, targeted attacks, a pre-pentest that moves into a full penetration test, and more.

How fast do you notify us?

At once, through the channel we agreed with you: email, Telegram or another one we set up together. We confirm that the finding has reached you, and only then add it to the report, so your team can start fixing the risk immediately.

What does the report look like?

There are two kinds. The portal report is always available, in seven versions: for IT and security teams, for analysts, for the business, for compliance, a four-page executive version, the full report and one you assemble yourself. It is set to your country, with the cost of an average breach and the laws that apply; the cost figures are estimates, updated periodically. Our algorithms build it from every re-checked record, the password reuse found and the statistics, with a decision for each finding (fix now, this week, can wait), its evidence and the fix. The same findings are available through the API.

On Enterprise, analysts also write a report for your company. Each one is unique to the company and closes the goals you set. It comes in two versions, one for the business and one for the IT team and analysts. It assesses your company the way an attacker would: how they would get in and what the impact would be. Then it sets out the defence: which employees need security awareness training on digital hygiene and passwords, what software to install, what to stop doing and which mistakes to avoid. All of this advice is built on your leaked data.
