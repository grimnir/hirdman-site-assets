New since 4.0From a record to a person

Version 4.0 put brand protection, asset management and an append-only log into the cabinet. Releases 4.1 and 4.2 followed. The six changes that matter most to a security team:

A dossier on every exposed person 4.2
One page per login: accounts, shared passwords, leaks by month, the personal data the leak sources hold (masked) and what to do, in order. A stealer log turns it red and puts “clean the computer” first.
A file you can hand over 4.2
The dossier downloads as one HTML file that opens offline, runs no script and prints on A4. Without passwords by default; with them only sealed by a one-time password. Every download is logged first.
A watchlist for the people who matter 4.1
Add the director, finance and admins. When an import brings one of them a new leak, the bell rings once, without a login or a password in the note.
Reports for one domain, and people at risk 4.1
Build a leaks report for a single domain or subsidiary. The Full report now lists the people whose one password opens two corporate sites or more.
An attestation sheet to sign 4.1
The full and regulator editions of the Brand report end with an attestation that repeats what was not checked. The short edition fits two A4 sheets.
A failed check says so 4.1
A breach check that got no answer used to read “0 found”. It now reads “failed: no answer”, and the record panel says when its sources could not be refreshed.

OverviewAll the work is visible: to you and to the regulator

Since version one we have looked at a company the way an attacker sees it. Leaked logins and passwords are only part of the picture, so we also check website components, open ports, forgotten services and look-alikes of your brand: continuous reconnaissance ahead of a pentest.

Regulators now ask companies to show the protection work they have done. HirdmanUI 4 is built for that request.

  • Every step is recorded. Findings, decisions by Hirdman analysts and by your team, evidence, exports and requests go into append-only logs. History stays intact from the very first entry.
  • History, like on maps. Every few days we take a snapshot of the website, its fingerprint and the perimeter. Any past snapshot opens “as it was then”: if port 80 was once open, history shows when it was found, who did what, and when it closed.
  • We do the heavy lifting. Analytics, checks and expertise are on the Hirdman side. Your team agrees, disputes or leaves a note.
  • A report for every reader. Brand reports come in three editions: full, a two-page brief for management and a regulator edition. Leak reports offer seven profiles: technical, analyst, business, compliance, executive, full and custom.

How it works

  1. Your namesYour domains and brand names. You control the list in the cabinet.
  2. Look-alike searchTyposquatting is one of 35+ techniques: look-alike characters across scripts, keyboard-layout and phonetic distance, token insertion and reordering, other zones, your brand plus an industry word, and the day's fresh registrations.
  3. Certificates in real timeOur own Certificate Transparency service streams every new certificate. A certificate issued for your name is caught the moment it appears, even if the site lives a couple of days.
  4. Measuring every nameMail, website, screenshot, favicon, registration, network, redirect chains, shared certificates and DNS infrastructure. 76 signals per name.
  5. ScoringRules and a local model on our server. Every conclusion rests on specific facts of the record.
  6. AnalystA person reviews the disputed cases, confirms them and writes down the grounds.
  7. Your cabinetA short queue of decisions, the evidence and ready-made reports.

What you see in the cabinet

  • Evidence wall. The look-alike next to your real website, with the reason under each one. A decision on a group is recorded for each record separately.
  • A verdict with grounds. “Action required”, “Monitor” or “Unrelated”, and the register splits into “Act now”, “Keep watching”, “Not assessed” and “Closed”. A human decision lies on top of the pipeline's score, and the original score stays visible.
  • Grouped by owner. Group a bin by registrar, address or icon to see one operator's names together. Urgency reads in words: Highest, High, Medium, Low.
  • Resolving or not, with a date. Every card says “Resolving · checked 23 Oct 2026”, “Not resolving” or “Not measured”, from the cycle that checked it.
  • Coverage per method. Above the registry you see how many records each method measured, and a missed source is named plainly.
  • Cycles with history. Any past cycle opens “as it was then”, and the feed shows what appeared since the last one.

CasesFrom a mention to a takedown

We follow mentions of your brand: social media, messengers, app stores, marketplaces, ads. Automation finds them, an analyst checks every finding by hand, and a confirmed finding becomes a case. A mobile app we analyze down to the code: what was changed and why. We have already taken several mobile app cases all the way to takedown.

  1. FoundAutomation spots the asset, an analyst confirms it by hand.
  2. AnalyzedWe take the asset apart: for an app, down to the code.
  3. FindingsWhat it does, whom it targets, why it matters and the evidence behind it.
  4. Takedown sentThe request goes to the store, registrar or platform.
  5. Taken downWe confirm the asset is gone and close the case.
  • One evidence list. Proofs filed by hand and captures from the pipeline in one list, each with its hash and capture date. Filter the registers by “With a capture”, “Link only” or “Nothing attached”.
  • A package your lawyers can verify. One ZIP per case or up to 50 cases at once, current files only or with history, readable file names, a README and a SHA-256 manifest.
  • Your team in the loop. “Reviewed”, “Sent to lawyers” and “Question for Hirdman” marks right in the case. The Cases button counts the files nobody on your side has downloaded yet.
  • Ready for the regulator. The whole case shows what was found, what was done and how it ended; the Brand report draws each case with its first screenshot.

Asset managementYour perimeter through an attacker's eyes

The Domains section is one inventory of your external infrastructure: names, addresses, ports, who runs them, technologies and the date of the last check. The first line answers first: the number that needs a person is a button that opens it.

  • Change feed. What appeared, disappeared and changed since the last cycle. Routine address changes at a provider are folded and counted.
  • Certificates and terms. What has expired or expires within 30 days, plus the registration terms of your domains.
  • Mail protection. SPF, DMARC and MTA-STS of your domains.
  • Open cloud storage on your perimeter that anyone can read.
  • Third parties on your perimeter. When a service on your address presents itself as another organization, you learn who else works in your infrastructure.
  • Checked and fine. A green mark for what is checked and a separate group for addresses with no response: every line says what exactly is known.

  • “Hand to IT”. People from a chosen risk group go out in one file: a line per person, websites by name, no passwords.

FAQQuestions we hear first

Who does the heavy lifting?

We do. Collection, ETL, deduplication, verification and risk scoring run on our side, on our own servers and with no public AI services. Your cabinet receives data that is already processed: sorted, assessed and tied to the people and systems it affects. We hand you the triaged result and a report you can act on the same day.

We are on 4.0. What does the update cost us?

The cabinet is offline for about 20 to 45 seconds on 200,000 records, 75 seconds at worst, after a verified safety copy. The first update to 4.2 adds a one-time check of the stored raw leak lines. Users, passwords, 2FA, records, API keys and settings carry over as they are.

Can we send a dossier outside the company?

Yes, that is what the file is for. By default it carries no passwords; a file with passwords is sealed with a password shown once. Every download is logged before the file is saved.

What does the regulator see?

A report on the work done: what was found, what was decided, by whom, when and on what grounds, closed by an attestation sheet. The logs are append-only, and any past state of the perimeter opens as it was then.
