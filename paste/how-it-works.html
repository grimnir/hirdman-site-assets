What happens after you send us a domain

Every step runs on our side, starting with the first look at your perimeter. Out of a large mass of varied data, your portal gets a structured list in priority order; on Enterprise our analysts also write a PDF report for you.

How an engagement runs

The free check and the proof of concept come before any contract, and a domain is all they need from your team.

  1. 01

    Free check request

    You send a domain or a question by email. The check runs entirely on our side, before any contract. Even before that, the self-service scan at scan.hirdman.pro gives a preliminary picture: a quick surface check against a small part of our database. Reverse checks come with the analysts.

  2. 02

    Pre-pentest: the perimeter as an attacker sees it

    From your domain we find every related project, including the ones on other domains, so shadow IT comes into view. Then we map subdomains as completely as possible, along with open ports and exposed or forgotten services.

  3. 03

    The cheapest way in

    We repeat the first stage of an attack: the path that needs the least effort. We look at leaked credentials and their variations, password and digital hygiene, and the habits of the people behind the accounts.

  4. 04

    Processing on our side

    We link the records into people, habits, patterns and weak points with our own ETL and local models, on hardware we run. How we handle your data

  5. 05

    NDA and proof of concept

    Under an NDA we show you the proof of concept on your own data and agree on what you need. Non-standard tasks are part of the work: a risk score for a client or counterparty before a payout, an internal incident investigation, identifying an insider.

  6. 06

    Contract and your portal

    After the contract we deploy your portal on a server you choose and load the processed data into it. The setup is ours. The plan sets how deep the processing goes and how detailed the recommendations are. Your license stays the same whatever the number of assets, and exports are unlimited.

  7. 07

    Report and ongoing work

    You receive a report with priorities, risk assessment and step-by-step recommendations, and confirmed risks at once through the agreed channel. The first picture comes within about half an hour; a proof-of-concept surface report takes 1 to 2 days.

The monitoring cycle, stage by stage

Hirdman analysts run each of the seven stages. The questions below go into sources, dates and alerts.

  1. 01

    Collect

    Closed forums, infostealer logs, private sources, dark web, messenger channels and breach markets, early in the data lifecycle.

  2. 02

    Verify

    Age, origin, duplicates. We filter out generated and repackaged sets and keep the breach date and the source date apart, with a confidence level.

  3. 03

    Reverse check

    From one identifier to the linked accounts across services. Rounds continue until the full picture is clear.

  4. 04

    Reconnaissance

    Related projects on other domains, subdomains, open ports, exposed and forgotten services, shadow IT: a pre-pentest, with analysts examining what the scanner finds.

  5. 05

    Prioritize

    Risk tiers weighted by account type, infostealer trace and password strength. Each finding leaves with a decision.

  6. 06

    Notify

    We report confirmed risks at once through the agreed channel. On Enterprise, VIP accounts are checked every hour.

  7. 07

    Remediate and re-check

    We verify each fix and keep the case open until the path is closed.

What raises the priority

Three signals move a finding up the list: a corporate account, a record from an infostealer, and a weak or reused password. Every finding leaves with one of three decisions.

Now

Fix now

The finding gives an attacker a way in today: an infostealer record, an administrative login, a live corporate password. We report it at once through the agreed channel.

This week

Fix this week

A confirmed exposure of a corporate account without an immediate path. It goes into the week's change list with the fix attached.

Can wait

Safe to defer

Old, personal or already closed records that still belong in the picture. We keep them in the report for completeness; action can wait.

Questions we hear first

Do we need to install anything?

For the free check and the proof of concept, a domain is enough. Under contract we deploy your portal on a server you choose and load the processed data into it; the setup is ours. Collection and processing stay on our side.

How soon do we see the first results?

The first picture is clear within about half an hour: how well the company is protected, what its digital hygiene is like and whether security awareness is in place. For a proof of concept, the surface report takes 1 to 2 days; the full work depends on the plan and the task.

We report a confirmed risk at once through the agreed channel.

Will you try to log in with a leaked credential?

Only with your written permission. Analysts examine leaked credentials and exposed subdomains. An access attempt against a critical service happens only when you have agreed to it, and only inside the agreed scope.

Where does the data come from?

Thousands of sources: public and private breach databases, closed and dark web forums, data markets, messenger channels, ransomware group sites and infostealer logs. We also follow news sites, leak aggregators and professional security and DLP channels, where a compromise is often mentioned first. When a breach is rumored, or a hacker group becomes active around a geopolitical event, we search for it deliberately across every source we reach.

We take data early, soon after it appears on semi-private forums and before it is repackaged and mixed with generated records. Some sources are closed, and underground partners often deliver new leaks and logs first. We download every set, validate it, clean out fakes and normalize it before it enters monitoring.

What can a leak with no password, or a hashed one, tell an attacker?

A great deal. Besides the 106 billion records in our database we hold 46 billion raw records, and they often carry mail correspondence, documents and personal details even where the password is missing or hashed. Put together, they make a digital profile of an employee: the picture attackers spend weeks assembling before a targeted attack.

We build that picture first and assess how exposed each employee is: what an attacker would learn by running reconnaissance on that person, and how open they are to targeted phishing or an attack through the support desk.

Do you cover infostealer logs?

Yes, and they carry a label of their own, because the risk is of another kind. A stealer log almost always means the whole workstation was compromised by malware. An attacker can then skip the sign-in and use a stolen session token or a session that is still valid.

That is why stealer records get extra response steps, and we use them in the reverse check and, under contract, in tests of how well your MFA holds.

What exactly do you monitor?

Your domains, the email patterns of your staff and your brand terms, which also feed the reverse check as keywords. On Enterprise we also watch specific addresses in VIP mode: executives, administrators, infrastructure engineers and other critical roles get priority analysis and a faster update cycle.

We watch your subdomains and infrastructure too, and keep ports and the full URL structure, including non-standard paths and service interfaces. One domain on different ports can serve anything from the public site to an admin panel, and leaks often hold direct links to such panels. Even behind a login, the exact address of an admin interface makes a targeted attack much easier, so we count it as a possible way in. Why subdomains matter.

How do you determine the date of a breach?

Only the attacker knows the real date of a breach, and even they can be unsure: attackers who plant a web shell sometimes find someone was already inside, and intruders can stay in a company for years unnoticed. So we date a finding by when it appeared in the space we monitor, and keep that date apart from any claimed breach date. Each date comes with a confidence level, so your team knows how firm it is.

How fresh is the data?

Processing runs around the clock. When new data matches your domains, email patterns or other indicators, our team gets an alert at once and checks the finding; once it is confirmed, we tell you immediately through the agreed channel. On Enterprise, VIP accounts are also checked every hour.

A breach rarely surfaces right after the compromise: attackers usually use the data themselves first, so days or months can pass before it appears. Shadow markets also carry announcements of databases for sale that are still unpublished, private sales to chosen buyers and fake or partly forged sets, which need extra analysis to tell a real leak from a scam.

How do alerts arrive?

In your portal and through the API. We report a confirmed risk at once through the official agreed channel. Alerting comes first and the report follows: the detailed write-up, with the source, the nature of the leak, the risks and what to do, comes after we have told you. On Enterprise, VIP accounts are checked every hour.

In one case we found a dangerous library and proof of an attack on a new client subdomain on a Sunday, the day it appeared. We told the client at once, and they started fixing it the same day; the report followed on Monday. Read the case.

Is this a platform or a managed service?

It is both: a self-service portal with analysts behind it. On Enterprise the main product is a structured report written by our analysts for your company, and on the other levels analysts handle the priority findings.
