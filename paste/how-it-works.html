How we work

From a domain to a confirmed fix.

We do the work on our side, from the first look at your perimeter to the fix. Your portal receives processed data and decisions.

Engagement

From request to report

Seven steps. The free check and the proof of concept come before any contract and ask nothing of your team.

  1. Free check requestYou send a domain or a question by email. No contract and nothing to install at this stage. A self-service scan at scan.hirdman.pro gives a preliminary picture even before that: a quick surface check on a small part of our database, without the reverse check.
  2. Pre-pentest: the perimeter as an attacker sees itFrom your domain we find every related project, including the ones on other domains, so shadow IT comes into view. Then we map subdomains as completely as possible, along with open ports and exposed or forgotten services.
  3. The cheapest way inWe repeat the first stage of an attack: the path that needs the least effort. Leaked credentials and their variations, password and digital hygiene, the habits of the people behind the accounts.
  4. Processing on our sideOur ETL and local models on our own hardware link the records into people, habits, patterns and weak points. No public AI services and no third-party clouds.
  5. NDA and proof of conceptUnder an NDA we show you the proof of concept on your own data and agree on what you need. Non-standard tasks are part of the work: a risk score for a client or counterparty before a payout, an internal incident investigation, identifying an insider.
  6. Contract and your portalAfter the contract we deploy your portal on a server you choose and load the processed data into it. The setup is ours. The plan sets how deep the processing goes and how detailed the recommendations are. No per-asset licenses and no export limits.
  7. Report and ongoing workYou receive a report with priorities, risk assessment and step-by-step recommendations, and confirmed risks at once through the agreed channel. The first picture comes within about half an hour; a proof-of-concept surface report takes 1 to 2 days.
Full cycle

Inside the cycle

Seven stages, every one of them run by Hirdman analysts. Nothing is left on your desk.

01

Collect

Closed forums, infostealer logs, private sources, dark web, messenger channels and breach markets, early in the data lifecycle.

02

Verify

Age, origin, duplicates. Generated and repackaged sets are filtered out. Breach date and source date are kept apart, with a confidence level.

03

Reverse check

From one identifier to the linked accounts across services. Rounds continue until the full picture is clear.

04

Reconnaissance

Related projects on other domains, subdomains, open ports, exposed and forgotten services, shadow IT: a pre-pentest examined by analysts rather than by a scanner alone.

05

Prioritize

Risk tiers weighted by account type, infostealer trace and password strength. Each finding leaves with a decision.

06

Notify

Confirmed risks reported at once through the agreed channel. On Enterprise, VIP accounts are checked every hour.

07

Remediate and re-check

Each fix is verified. The case stays open until the path is closed.

Questions

Questions we hear first

Do we need to install anything?

Not for the free check or the proof of concept: a domain is enough. Under contract we deploy your portal on a server you choose and load the processed data into it; the setup is ours. Collection and processing stay on our side.

How soon do we see the first results?

The first picture is clear within about half an hour: how well the company is protected, what its digital hygiene is like and whether security awareness is in place. For a proof of concept, the surface report takes 1 to 2 days; the full work depends on the plan and the task.

A confirmed risk is reported at once through the agreed channel.

Will you try to log in with a leaked credential?

Only with your written permission. Analysts examine leaked credentials and exposed subdomains. An access attempt against a critical service happens only when you have agreed to it, and only inside the agreed scope.

Where does the data come from?

Thousands of sources: public and private breach databases, closed and dark web forums, data markets, messenger channels, ransomware group sites and infostealer logs. We also follow news sites, leak aggregators and professional security and DLP channels, where a compromise is often mentioned first. When a breach is rumored, or a hacker group becomes active around a geopolitical event, we search for it deliberately across every source we reach.

We take data early, soon after it appears on semi-private forums and before it is repackaged and mixed with generated records. Some sources are closed, and underground partners often deliver new leaks and logs first. Every set is downloaded, validated, cleaned of fakes and normalized before it enters monitoring.

What can a leak with no password, or a hashed one, tell an attacker?

A great deal. Besides the 106 billion records in our database we hold 46 billion raw records, and they often carry mail correspondence, documents and personal details even where the password is missing or hashed. Put together, they make a digital profile of an employee: the picture attackers spend weeks assembling before a targeted attack.

We build that picture first and assess how exposed each employee is: what an attacker would learn by running reconnaissance on that person, and how open they are to targeted phishing or an attack through the support desk.

Do you cover infostealer logs?

Yes, and they carry a label of their own, because the risk is of another kind. A stealer log almost always means the whole workstation was compromised by malware. An attacker can then skip the sign-in and use a stolen session token or a session that is still valid.

That is why stealer records get extra response steps, and we use them in the reverse check and, under contract, in tests of how well your MFA holds.

What exactly do you monitor?

Your domains, the email patterns of your staff and your brand terms, which also feed the reverse check as keywords. On Enterprise we also watch specific addresses in VIP mode: executives, administrators, infrastructure engineers and other critical roles get priority analysis and a faster update cycle.

We watch your subdomains and infrastructure too, and keep ports and the full URL structure, including non-standard paths and service interfaces. One domain on different ports can serve anything from the public site to an admin panel, and leaks often hold direct links to such panels. Even behind a login, the exact address of an admin interface makes a targeted attack much easier, so we count it as a possible way in. Why subdomains matter.

How do you determine the date of a breach?

Only the attacker knows the real date of a breach, and not always even they: attackers who plant a web shell sometimes find someone was already inside, and intruders can stay in a company for years without being noticed. So we date a finding by when it appeared in the space we monitor, and keep that date apart from any claimed breach date. Each date comes with a confidence level, so your team knows how firm it is.

How fresh is the data?

Processing runs around the clock. When new data matches your domains, email patterns or other indicators, our team is alerted at once and checks the finding; once it is confirmed, you are told immediately through the agreed channel. On Enterprise, VIP accounts are also checked every hour.

A breach rarely surfaces right after the compromise: attackers usually use the data themselves first, so days or months can pass before it appears. Shadow markets also carry announcements of databases for sale that are not yet published, private sales to chosen buyers and fake or partly forged sets, which need extra analysis to tell a real leak from a scam.

How do alerts arrive?

In your portal and through the API, and a confirmed risk is reported at once through the official agreed channel. Alerting comes first and the report follows: the detailed write-up, with the source, the nature of the leak, the risks and what to do, comes after you have been told. On Enterprise, VIP accounts are checked every hour.

In one case we found a dangerous library and proof of an attack on a new client subdomain on a Sunday, the day it appeared. The client was told at once and started fixing it the same day; the report followed on Monday. Read the case.

Is this a platform or a managed service?

It is both: a self-service portal with analysts behind it. On Enterprise the main product is a structured report written by our analysts for your company, and on the other levels analysts handle the priority findings.
