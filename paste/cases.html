Cases

Four doors we found open.

Hirdman has worked with over 300 companies across industries including banks, fintech, government and IT. Four engagements, anonymized, show what one exposure opens and how the finding reached the client. A fifth shows the same data at work in an internal investigation.

Fintech

An active credential with high-privilege access

A private leak, not a public dump, contained an active credential of a current employee. The account gave high-privilege access to the company's project-management tool.

ContextA fintech company. The record surfaced in a private leak.
DiscoveryAn active employee credential with high-privilege access to a project-management tool.
OutcomeReported to the client with a prioritized remediation plan.
Healthcare

One administrator account, every website, patient data

An administrator account of a healthcare corporation surfaced with full access to all company websites and to patient data. Analysts confirmed the access in 30 seconds.

ContextA healthcare corporation. All company websites and patient data sat behind one administrator account.
DiscoveryThe account was exposed, and the access was confirmed in 30 seconds.
OutcomeReported to the client with a prioritized remediation plan.
Link management

A compromised account that could redirect links

A link-management service, whose links sit in emails, posts and documents. One account was compromised; attackers could have replaced legitimate links with phishing URLs.

ContextA link-management service. Its links are followed on trust.
DiscoveryA compromised account. Legitimate links could have been replaced with phishing URLs.
OutcomeReported to the client with a prioritized remediation plan.
Attack-surface reconnaissance

Keys to production in a public file

A UI development tool ran on two of the client's subdomains. Its build compiled the environment file into the public bundle: 120+ variables in one file and 711 across all bundles, with tokens for repositories, cloud roles, databases and storage.

ContextNo leaked emails or passwords on the domain. A monitoring feed alone would have shown nothing.
DiscoveryTokens readable by anyone who opened the page. One token confirmed repository access.
OutcomeThe client was notified at once and secret rotation started right after.

The pattern

What these cases have in common.

A minor exposure escalates

One credential, one account, one environment file. Each looked small on its own. Each led to the core: a privileged tool, patient data, production tokens.

The door does not matter to an attacker

A leaked credential, a compromised account, a file in a public page. The attacker takes whichever door is open. Each finding is ranked by what it opens.

Monitoring and penetration testing cover two different doors

Three cases begin with an exposed account. The fourth had no leaked password; the exposure sat on the perimeter, where a penetration tester looks. Watching one door leaves the other open.

Internal investigations

The same data finds the insider.

URLs, logins and passwords from leaks show where a company is exposed. They also serve an internal investigation: who created a password, where it travelled and whose accounts it opens. We run this work together with the client's security team, under an NDA, starting from the data they provide.

Internal investigation · published anonymously with the client's permission

One password tied two insiders to a hacker forum

The client's security team suspected that an employee was selling the company's source code. One employee had generated a complex password and passed it to an accomplice, a second insider. The same password was then used to sign in to a hacker forum. A password that rare, found in every link of the chain, became one of the markers that the accounts belonged together and were used with intent against the company.

ContextSuspected sale of source code by an employee. The work ran with the client's security team under an NDA.
MethodThe unique password followed through the leaks, then OSINT, leak and infostealer data on the people involved, their forum posts and social-network correspondence, correlated into one chain.
DiscoveryA direct link: the employee was in contact on Telegram with a second insider inside the company.
OutcomeThe full chain, with evidence for each link, went to the client. It helped identify both insiders, and the case went on to criminal proceedings.
