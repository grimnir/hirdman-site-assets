Four exposures we found at client companies

Hirdman has worked with more than 300 companies across industries including banks, fintech, government and IT. Four engagements, anonymized, show what one exposure opens and how the finding reached the client. A fifth shows the same data at work in an internal investigation.

Fintech

An active credential with high-privilege access

A private leak contained an active credential of a current employee. The account gave high-privilege access to the company's project-management tool.

Outcome
We passed the active credential and a remediation plan ranked by priority to the client's security team as fast as we could. The team took them into its process and closed the access.
Insider
The project-management tool ran on a subdomain outside the agreed scope. An insider at the company had set it up, and an investigation followed: who set it up, how, and what information was copied there.

Healthcare

One administrator account, every website, patient data

An administrator account of a healthcare corporation surfaced with full access to all company websites and to patient data. Analysts confirmed the access in 30 seconds.

Outcome
The confirmed access and a remediation plan went to the client's security team at once. The team took them into its process and closed the one account that stood in front of every company website and the patient data.

Link management

A compromised account that could redirect links

A link-management service, whose links sit in emails, posts and documents. One account was compromised; attackers could have replaced legitimate links with phishing URLs.

Context
People follow the service's links on trust.
Outcome
We handed the compromised account, the phishing risk to those links and a remediation plan to the client's security team without delay. The team worked them through its own process and fixed them.

Attack-surface reconnaissance

Keys to production in a public file

A UI development tool ran on two of the client's subdomains. Its build compiled the environment file into the public bundle: 711 variables across all bundles, more than 120 of them in one file, with tokens for repositories, cloud roles, databases and storage.

Context
The domain had zero leaked emails or passwords; attack-surface reconnaissance is what found the exposure.
Discovery
Tokens readable by anyone who opened the page. One token confirmed repository access.
Outcome
We notified the client at once, and secret rotation started right after.

What the four cases share

One credential, one account and one environment file each looked small on its own, and each led to the core: a privileged tool, patient data and production tokens. A leaked credential, a compromised account and a file in a public page are all ways in, so we rank each finding by what it reaches.

Three cases begin with an exposed account. In the fourth, the domain had zero leaked passwords, and the exposure sat on the perimeter, where a penetration tester looks. Monitoring watches the leaks, penetration testing watches the perimeter, and both need watching.

Internal investigations

The same leak data helps identify an insider

URLs, logins and passwords from leaks show where a company is exposed. They also serve an internal investigation: who created a password, where it travelled and whose accounts it opens. We run this work together with the client's security team, under an NDA, starting from the data they provide.

Internal investigation, published anonymously with the client's permission

One password tied two insiders to a hacker forum

The client's security team suspected that an employee was selling the company's source code. One employee had generated a complex password and passed it to an accomplice, a second insider. The same password was then used to sign in to a hacker forum. A password that rare, found in every link of the chain, became one of the markers that the accounts belonged together and were used with intent against the company.

Context
Suspected sale of source code by an employee. The work ran with the client's security team under an NDA.
Method
We followed the unique password through the leaks, then correlated OSINT, leak and infostealer data on the people involved, their forum posts and social-network correspondence into one chain.
Discovery
A direct link: the employee was in contact on Telegram with a second insider inside the company.
Outcome
We gave the client the full chain, with evidence for each link. It helped identify both insiders, and the case went on to criminal proceedings.
